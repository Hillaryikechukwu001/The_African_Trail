Kenya is the birthplace of humanity — with fossils in the Rift Valley dating back millions of years. Today it is also the birthplace of a new kind of tourism: one rooted in conservation, community, and a deep respect for the land and its people.

01
Wildlife Reserve

Masai Mara National Reserve

The Masai Mara is arguably the greatest wildlife spectacle on earth. Named after the Maasai people who have inhabited the land for centuries, the reserve covers 1,510 square kilometres of open grassland and is home to the "Big Five" year-round. Between July and October, over 1.5 million wildebeest, zebras, and gazelles thunder across the Mara River in the Great Migration — a natural event that has no equal on the planet.

Beyond the wildlife, the Mara is inseparable from Maasai culture. Visiting community conservancies on the reserve's edges connects travellers to a way of life refined over centuries — cattle herding, beadwork, warrior traditions, and a deep spiritual relationship with the land that modern conservation now depends on.

  • Location Narok County, Rift Valley
  • Best time to visit July – October (migration)
  • Entry USD 80 adults (non-residents)
02
UNESCO Biosphere Reserve

Amboseli National Park

Amboseli offers one of Africa's most iconic vistas: a herd of free-roaming elephants silhouetted against the snow-capped peak of Mount Kilimanjaro, rising 5,895 metres across the Tanzanian border. This park is home to some of the largest and most studied elephant populations in Africa, with family herds that researchers have tracked across generations.

The park's swamps, fed by underground springs from Kilimanjaro's glaciers, attract enormous concentrations of wildlife in the dry season. Amboseli is also central to efforts to rebuild trust between Maasai communities and conservation bodies — a model for community-based wildlife management being adopted across the continent.

  • Location Kajiado County, southern Kenya
  • Best time to visit June – October
  • Entry USD 60 adults (non-residents)
03
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Lamu Old Town

Founded in the 14th century, Lamu Old Town is one of the oldest and best-preserved Swahili settlements in East Africa. Its narrow coral-stone streets, carved wooden doors, and rooftop terraces have remained virtually unchanged for seven centuries — a living museum of a maritime civilisation that once connected the African coast to Arabia, Persia, and the Indian subcontinent.

No motor vehicles operate in Lamu town — donkeys and wooden dhows remain the primary modes of transport. The Lamu Cultural Festival, held annually, celebrates Swahili poetry, dhow racing, and the traditions of a people whose identity is woven equally from African, Arab, and Indian threads.

  • Location Lamu County, Kenyan coast
  • Best time to visit June – September
  • Entry Free (museum KES 500)
04
Cultural Institution

Nairobi National Museum

The Nairobi National Museum is Kenya's premier cultural institution, housing an extraordinary collection that spans palaeontology, natural history, ethnography, and contemporary African art. The museum's collection of hominid fossils — including the famous skull of Homo habilis found by Richard Leakey at Lake Turkana — anchors Kenya's place in the story of human evolution.

The adjoining Snake Park, the Gallery of Kenyan History, and the rotating exhibitions of modern Kenyan artists make this a destination that rewards repeated visits. The museum grounds also hold the Joy Adamson Gallery, celebrating the author of Born Free and Kenya's relationship with its big cats.

  • Location Museum Hill, Nairobi
  • Best time to visit Year-round
  • Entry KES 1,200 adults
