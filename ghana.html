From the echoes of the transatlantic slave trade along its shores to the golden stools of the Ashanti Kingdom — Ghana carries history with pride, dignity, and an unmatched spirit of welcome.

01
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Cape Coast Castle

Built by the Swedish in 1653 and later controlled by the British, Cape Coast Castle stands as one of the most significant monuments to the transatlantic slave trade. Its dungeons held hundreds of thousands of enslaved Africans before they were shipped across the Atlantic — a harrowing history that draws African diaspora from around the world in search of roots, reconciliation, and remembrance.

The castle also houses the Cape Coast Museum, which documents the region's pre-colonial history, the Fante people, and centuries of coastal trade. Walking through the "Door of No Return" remains one of the most emotionally powerful experiences on the African continent.

  • Location Cape Coast, Central Region
  • Best time to visit November – March
  • Entry GHS 120 (foreign visitors)
02
Tropical Rainforest

Kakum National Park

Spanning over 375 square kilometres of pristine tropical rainforest, Kakum is Ghana's most visited national park. Its famous seven-bridge canopy walkway — suspended 40 metres above the forest floor — offers a breathtaking journey through the treetops, providing views of forest elephants, bongo antelopes, and over 400 bird species found nowhere else on earth.

The park sits within an ancient forest ecosystem that has remained undisturbed for thousands of years, harbouring rare plants with medicinal and cultural significance to local communities. It is one of only three places in Africa where you can walk above a rainforest canopy.

  • Location Central Region, near Cape Coast
  • Best time to visit December – March
  • Entry GHS 80 adults
03
Wildlife Safari

Mole National Park

Ghana's largest wildlife sanctuary covers nearly 5,000 square kilometres of savannah, woodland, and wetlands in the Northern Region. Home to over 93 mammal species — including West Africa's largest elephant population — Mole offers guided walking safaris that put you within metres of elephants at the watering holes below Mole Motel's famous viewing deck.

Unlike East Africa's heavily touristed parks, Mole retains a raw, unhurried quality. Buffalo, roan antelope, waterbuck, and baboons roam freely alongside the elephants, while over 300 bird species make it a paradise for birdwatchers. Local Dagomba guides bring deep cultural and ecological knowledge to every trail.

  • Location Northern Region, Damongo
  • Best time to visit November – April
  • Entry GHS 60 adults
04
Cultural Heritage

Kumasi Cultural Centre

The cultural heart of the Ashanti Kingdom, Kumasi is Ghana's second city and one of the most historically rich destinations in West Africa. The Kumasi Cultural Centre — home to the National Cultural Centre, Okomfo Anokye Sword site, and the Manhyia Palace Museum — offers an immersive window into the world of the Asantehene and the 300-year-old Ashanti empire.

Kente weaving demonstrations, woodcarving exhibitions, and the legendary Kejetia Market — the largest open-air market in West Africa — make Kumasi an extraordinary sensory and cultural experience. The nearby Ashanti villages offer guided tours into living traditions of royalty, spiritual practice, and craftsmanship that persist to this day.

  • Location Ashanti Region, Kumasi
  • Best time to visit October – March
  • Entry GHS 50 adults
