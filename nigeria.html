Nigeria is a continent compressed into one country. The Yoruba kingdoms of the southwest, the Igbo traditions of the southeast, the Hausa-Fulani emirate system of the north, and hundreds of other cultures coexist to produce a nation of staggering creative and cultural depth — one that has given the world Afrobeats, Nollywood, and some of the greatest bronze artworks in history.

01
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove

The Osun-Osogbo Sacred Grove is one of the last remaining sacred groves in Yorubaland and is considered the abode of Osun, the Yoruba goddess of fertility, water, and love. Stretching along the banks of the Osun River in Osun State, this dense forest sanctuary is studded with shrines, sculptures, and artworks dedicated to Osun and other Yoruba deities.

Every August, the Osun-Osogbo Festival draws hundreds of thousands of worshippers, tourists, and members of the global Yoruba diaspora to witness the ancient ritual in which the Arugba — a young virgin — carries a sacred calabash on her head in procession to the river. It is one of the most spiritually charged events in West Africa and a living testimony to the survival of indigenous African religion.

  • Location Osogbo, Osun State
  • Best time to visit August (Festival) or year-round
  • Entry NGN 2,000 adults
02
Wildlife Reserve

Yankari Game Reserve

Nigeria's premier wildlife destination, Yankari covers over 2,000 square kilometres of savannah and woodland in Bauchi State and is home to one of Nigeria's largest remaining elephant populations, as well as lions, hippos, baboons, and over 350 bird species. The park's famous Wikki Warm Springs — natural warm water springs that bubble up from the earth at a constant 31°C — are among the most extraordinary natural features in the country.

Yankari also preserves significant archaeological heritage: the Marshall Caves within the reserve contain some of the oldest known human habitation sites in West Africa. Guided game drives and walking safaris offer an authentic wilderness experience rarely found in West Africa's heavily populated landscapes.

  • Location Bauchi State, northeast Nigeria
  • Best time to visit November – April
  • Entry NGN 3,500 adults
03
Ancient Kingdom

Benin City & the Royal Palace

Benin City was once the capital of one of the most sophisticated civilisations in pre-colonial Africa. The Benin Kingdom produced bronze and ivory artworks of unparalleled technical and artistic mastery. The famous Benin Bronzes — looted by British forces in 1897 and now being repatriated from museums worldwide — rank among the greatest achievements in world art history.

The ancient walls of Benin once stretched over 16,000 kilometres and enclosed an area larger than medieval London — among the largest pre-mechanical earthworks ever constructed. The Oba's Palace and the Benin National Museum offer access to this extraordinary legacy, with ongoing repatriation making each visit richer than the last.

  • Location Benin City, Edo State
  • Best time to visit November – March
  • Entry NGN 1,500 adults (museum)
04
Cultural Heritage

Calabar Museum & Carnival

Calabar sits at the confluence of the Calabar and Great Kwa rivers and has served as a gateway between Nigeria and the wider world for centuries. The Old Residency Museum documents the history of the transatlantic slave trade from Nigeria's perspective alongside the rich culture of the Efik, Ejagham, and Bekwarra peoples.

Every December, Calabar hosts Africa's biggest street carnival — a month-long festival drawing over three million visitors with elaborate costumes, Afrobeats, traditional masquerades, and the famous Ekpe masquerade performances unique to the Cross River region. The nearby Cross River National Park, home to endangered Cross River gorillas, makes Calabar one of Nigeria's most rewarding travel destinations.

  • Location Calabar, Cross River State
  • Best time to visit December (Carnival)
  • Entry NGN 1,000 adults (museum)
