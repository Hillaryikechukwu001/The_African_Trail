01
Ancient Wonder of the World

The Pyramids of Giza

The only surviving wonder of the ancient world, the Pyramids of Giza have stood on the edge of the Sahara for over 4,500 years. Built as royal tombs for Pharaohs Khufu, Khafre, and Menkaure, the Great Pyramid of Khufu — originally 146 metres tall — remained the tallest man-made structure on earth for nearly 3,800 years. The precision of their construction, using over 2.3 million stone blocks, continues to astonish engineers and archaeologists today.

The adjacent Great Sphinx, carved from a single limestone outcrop and measuring 73 metres in length, has stood watch over the plateau for millennia. The Giza Plateau at sunset, when the monuments turn amber against the desert sky, is among the most profound visual experiences anywhere on earth.

  • Location Giza Plateau, Greater Cairo
  • Best time to visit October – April
  • Entry EGP 450 adults
02
Ancient City of Thebes

Luxor Temple & Karnak

Luxor has been called "the world's greatest open-air museum" — and no title is more earned. The city sits atop ancient Thebes, the capital of New Kingdom Egypt, and is home to an unrivalled concentration of temples, tombs, and monuments spanning 1,500 years of continuous construction. The Karnak Temple Complex alone took 2,000 years and 30 pharaohs to build.

Across the Nile lies the Valley of the Kings, where 63 royal tombs have been discovered, including the intact tomb of Tutankhamun unearthed by Howard Carter in 1922. The painted walls inside these tombs — depicting the journey of the soul through the afterlife — represent some of the most extraordinary art ever created by human hands.

  • Location Luxor, Upper Egypt
  • Best time to visit October – March
  • Entry EGP 360 (Luxor Temple)
03
World-Class Museum

The Egyptian Museum, Cairo

The Egyptian Museum in Tahrir Square houses the most important collection of ancient Egyptian artefacts in existence — over 120,000 objects spanning three millennia of civilisation. The museum's centrepiece is the Tutankhamun Gallery, displaying the treasures found inside his intact tomb: golden death masks, jewelled sarcophagi, chariots, and thousands of objects crafted for eternity.

The Royal Mummy Room contains the preserved remains of some of Egypt's most powerful pharaohs — including Ramesses II, Seti I, and Hatshepsut — allowing visitors to stand in the presence of rulers who shaped the ancient world. The Grand Egyptian Museum near Giza, opened in 2023, now houses even larger collections with state-of-the-art presentation.

  • Location Tahrir Square, Cairo
  • Best time to visit Year-round (early morning)
  • Entry EGP 300 adults
04
UNESCO World Heritage Site

Abu Simbel Temples

Carved entirely from a sandstone cliff by Ramesses II around 1264 BCE, the twin temples of Abu Simbel stand as the most audacious architectural achievement of the ancient world. The four colossal statues of Ramesses II guarding the Great Temple each stand 20 metres tall. Twice a year — on February 22 and October 22 — sunlight penetrates the inner sanctuary to illuminate the faces of the gods within.

In a feat of modern engineering to match the ancient one, the entire temple complex was relocated 65 metres uphill between 1964 and 1968 to save it from the rising waters of Lake Nasser. This UNESCO-led operation remains the largest archaeological rescue operation in history.

  • Location Aswan Governorate, southern Egypt
  • Best time to visit Oct – Feb (Feb 22 sun alignment)
  • Entry EGP 540 adults
